How to choose a packaging design partner.

The portfolio matters. The questions behind it matter more.

By Noah Barlow / Updated October 21, 2026 / 8 minute read

Smaller / Field note
Ask pastthe mockup.

Agency selection / Packaging / Brand systems

Written from the work, not from a content calendar.

A packaging partner should help make the choice clearer, the range more coherent and the final files easier to produce. Pretty is part of the job. It is not the whole job.

Ask to see the system, not one front panel.

A hero shot can hide a lot. Ask how the design works across flavours, formats, sizes and required information. Look for actual packs, secondary panels and range logic.

If every case study ends at a single perfect render, you still do not know whether the studio can build something your team can use.

Find out who is doing the work.

Who joins the first meeting? Who writes the strategy? Who designs the package? Who answers the printer? Ask whether the people selling the project stay in it.

A useful answer

The fewer handoffs between thinking and making, the less likely the idea gets diluted on the way to production.

Seven questions worth asking.

  • 01

    How do you connect positioning to the front-of-pack hierarchy?

  • 02

    How do you test the system across the full range?

  • 03

    What is your role in regulatory and bilingual planning?

  • 04

    Who owns final artwork, prepress and printer handoff?

  • 05

    What do you need from our team to keep decisions moving?

  • 06

    How do you handle a new SKU after launch?

  • 07

    Can you show a project that made it into the real world?

Read the proposal for responsibility.

Deliverables are useful. Responsibility is better. The proposal should make clear who supplies copy, who approves regulatory content, who provides dielines, how many SKUs are included and where the agency’s production role ends.

Ambiguity is usually what makes a tidy quote grow teeth later.

Look for a point of view you can work with.

You are not hiring a pair of hands. You are hiring judgement. The studio should be able to explain what it sees, what it recommends and what it would leave alone.

The relationship does not need theatre. It needs clear thinking, honest feedback and enough trust to choose a direction.

A simple shortlist test.

Strong work

Would you want your product beside it?

Relevant proof

Can they show systems that survived range, production and launch?

Clear process

Do you understand what happens next and who owns it?

Useful chemistry

Can the team challenge the brief without making the room exhausting?

Questions that come up.

Should a packaging design agency understand production?

Yes. The studio should be able to discuss dielines, print methods, proofing, finishes, colour behaviour and responsibility for final production artwork.

Is category experience required?

Relevant experience helps, but a strong process, systems thinking and evidence of learning a category can matter more than having designed the exact product before.

Who should be in the first agency meeting?

The people responsible for the business decision, the brand and the product. A small decision-making group creates a better brief and faster feedback.

What should a packaging portfolio prove?

It should show the idea, the hierarchy, the range, real production and how the system worked beyond one hero mockup.
