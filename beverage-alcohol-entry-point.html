Thinking / Beverage alcohol

The category has an entry problem.

Gen Z doesn’t need another lesson. It needs a way in.

Field note / 5 minute read

The short version

If the first interaction feels like a test, the brand has already made the choice harder.

Beverage alcohol keeps building brands for people who already know how the category works.

New drinkers arrive with different questions.

What is this? Is it for me? What will it taste like? Can I order it without looking like I studied for the wrong exam?

The category often answers with regions, rules, production methods and a family history. Interesting, eventually. Not especially helpful at the front door.

Make the first choice feel easy.

  1. 01
    Three-second clarity.

    Can someone understand the product without category knowledge?

  2. 02
    Zero homework.

    Does the brand work before someone learns the regions, styles or hierarchy?

  3. 03
    Visual first.

    If the copy disappeared, would the brand still make sense?

  4. 04
    Socially usable.

    Is it easy to order, bring and share without a speech?

  5. 05
    Identity before education.

    Lead with who it is for. Let heritage and process support the story instead of blocking the entrance.

The entry SKU is a welcome mat.

The best first product isn’t necessarily the cheapest one. It’s the one that makes participation feel obvious.

Approachability is not the same as dumbing the brand down. It means giving people a reason to come closer before asking them to care about the details.

The brand should open the door before it starts the lesson.

Would a 23-year-old choose it without help?
