Dish

A new retail behaviour nobody had a category for.

The problem

A new retail behaviour nobody had a category for.

The move

Sell famous restaurant dishes. Never “frozen food.”

What shipped

Identity, packaging, freezer, digital journey and launch.

What changed

A repeatable building-by-building experience ready to pilot.

01

The challenge

A new dinner habit, hiding in the lobby.

Michael came to us with an idea, not a company: put restaurant dishes in smart freezers inside condos. No delivery driver. No sad supermarket lasagna. Dinner, downstairs.

The tricky bit wasn’t the logo. It was making a brand-new habit feel obvious enough to try on a Tuesday night.

Not delivery

No driver, no wait and no mystery fee.

Not frozen grocery

A named dish from a restaurant you know.

Not a vending machine

Yes, there is hardware. No, dinner is not a snack.

02

Our role

We designed the whole Tuesday night.

The name on the freezer, the sleeve in your hand, the first-meal offer and the tap on your phone all had to answer the same question: “Do I trust this for dinner?”

Brand

Famous dishes from local restaurants.

Experience

Sleeves, freezer graphics, QR flow and chef stories.

Launch

Curiosity had to become a Tuesday habit.

03

Packaging system

The freezer door is a lousy salesperson.

Frosted glass, fluorescent light and about three seconds of attention. The pack had to make the restaurant, dish and value clear before the door even opened.

Meal sleeves

Restaurant and dish lead.

Information

Nutrition, price and reheating stay findable.

Production

A system that can scale across partners.

04

Go to market

Win one building. Then the next.

Toronto-wide awareness could wait. The smarter move was density: make the freezer useful in one building, learn, then repeat it at the next address.
