# Toronto agency economics research summary

Checked: 2026-08-06
Scope: Ten prominent agencies with a Toronto presence. This is an evidence audit and normalized operating-cost model, not a ranking of creative quality, revenue, profit, or value.

## Evidence standard

- **Reported:** published by the agency, a government body, landlord, or other primary source.
- **Observed:** visible in a public company profile or listing, but not necessarily audited or Toronto-specific.
- **Modelled:** calculated from a range of public signals and shared assumptions.
- **Assumed:** a normalized benchmark used to make unlike businesses comparable.

## Coverage score

The public-evidence coverage score is not a probability that a number is correct. It measures how much useful evidence was found across five weighted fields: entity/ownership (10), Toronto staffing (25), office footprint (20), role mix (20), and pricing (25). Partial evidence receives partial credit. A low score means the public record does not support precision.

## Shared model

Space need is not claimed leased area. It is the headcount range multiplied by 90–115 square feet per employee. The operating floor is `(loaded people cost + inferred occupancy + other operating cost) / collected delivery hours`. Inputs vary by agency type and scale within these cohort bounds: $112k–$150k loaded people cost, 66%–79% delivery share, 1,325–1,500 collected hours per delivery employee, $58–$82 gross occupancy per square foot, and other operating cost equal to 12%–21% of people cost. Profit, media, production pass-through and tax are excluded.

## Strongest findings

1. People and utilization dominate the model. At the default scenario, office cost is about four cents of each operating dollar.
2. Cutting collected hours from 1,425 to 1,325 raises the hourly floor by about 7.5%, while adding $20 per square foot to occupancy changes it by roughly $2 per delivery hour.
3. Actual rate cards are almost entirely private. The strongest public rate found is a City of Toronto record stating a $179 blended hourly rate in Cossette's original 2017 contract; it is real but stale and not comparable to a 2026 commercial scope.
4. Exact office square footage was not found for most agencies. Addresses are evidence of presence, not proof of leased area.

The cohort ranges sum to 1,640–2,905 modelled Toronto people and 147,600–334,075 square feet of workforce-equivalent space need. These totals communicate uncertainty; they are not a combined staff or lease claim.
