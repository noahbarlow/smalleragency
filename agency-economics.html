before an agency makes anything, it has to pay for itself.

We looked at ten prominent Toronto agencies and modelled the people, space and structure behind the rate card. Not to name a winner. To show what kind of machine a client is buying.

01The question

agency pricing is opaque. the cost structure doesn't have to be.

The useful question is not “who is cheapest?” It is “where does the money go before the work gets made?”

This is not a claim about any agency's revenue, lease, payroll or profit. It is a normalized scenario built from public company bands, visible addresses and current Toronto benchmarks.

02

People cost

$118k–$142k weighted annual employer cost, varying by agency mix. Salary, payroll burden and benefits included.

03

Space

90–115 square feet per Toronto employee and $68 per square foot gross occupancy, normalized from the Colliers Toronto office benchmark ↗. Neither is presented as a known lease term.

04

Utilization

71–77% delivery staff, 1,350–1,500 collected hours per delivery FTE, then 15% operating overhead. Profit is excluded.

precision is not the same thing as truth. a useful estimate shows its assumptions.

EstimatedDerived from observed data using the stated model.

AssumedA benchmark used to make unlike businesses comparable.

The point

buy the right machine for the problem.

Large infrastructure is useful when the problem needs it. When it doesn't, fewer layers can mean better conversations—and a more sensible invoice.
