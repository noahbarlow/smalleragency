the agency rate is not the price.

A $100,000 budget buys 500 hours at $200—or 400 hours at $250. The higher-rate team has to create the same outcome with 20% fewer hours.

That is the useful comparison. Then ask what those hours contain: senior thinking, hands-on making, coordination and the operating system around them.

buy the bench only when the brief needs it.

Scale is valuable when you need many disciplines, markets or production lanes at once. If the problem needs three senior makers, scale can become coordination instead of capability.

What the audit actually found

agencies show the work.
they rarely show the machine.

0/10

publish a current primary-source commercial rate card.

0/10

publish an audited Toronto role mix or delivery ratio.

0/10

disclose enough lease detail to calculate exact office overhead.

That means a precise agency-by-agency cost ranking would be theatre. The useful comparison is simpler: how large is the operating system, and how much of it does your problem need?

make the premium prove itself.

  1. 01

    Turn the fee into hours by role.

    A $250 rate can be good value. Show how many strategy, creative, design, production and account hours the budget actually buys.

  2. 02

    Name the people doing those hours.

    Separate hands-on makers from reviewers, coordinators and senior people who only appeared in the pitch.

  3. 03

    Explain what the premium removes.

    A higher rate should buy better judgment, faster decisions, less rework or lower risk—not simply another layer.

Model and source notes ＋

The $200 hour

The default scenario uses a round $200 planning rate—not a claimed market average—plus $132,000 in annual loaded people cost per employee, a 73% delivery share, 1,425 collected hours per delivery employee, 100 square feet per employee, $68 gross occupancy per square foot and other operating cost equal to 15% of people cost. The modelled cost floor is $153 per delivery hour.

The Toronto ranges

Ranges use public company bands, visible associated-profile counts, office structure and network allocation. They are intentionally broad. No agency supplied or verified the model.

Price evidence

The Toronto-specific signal is a City record ↗ documenting Cossette's $179 blended rate in a 2017 contract. Current public cards outside the cohort show the role premium: Crown Social ↗ publishes $200 blended and $250 for strategy/creative direction; SketchDeck ↗ publishes $250–$313 for Creative Director and $300–$375 for Senior Strategist. Several sources do not label currency, so these show rate architecture—not Toronto pricing.

The point

a higher rate should buy a shorter path to better work.

If it only buys more layers, it is overhead. If it buys judgment, speed and fewer wrong turns, it can be the cheaper choice.
