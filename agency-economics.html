01The question

the most visible overhead isn't the biggest one.

An expensive office is easy to picture. But in the normalized model, real estate is about four cents of the operating dollar. People mix, layers and unused capacity move the rate much more.

This is not a claim about any agency's revenue, lease, payroll, rate card or profit. It is an evidence audit plus a scenario model built from public company signals and Toronto benchmarks.

01

People range

Toronto ranges use public company bands, visible associated-profile counts, office structure and network allocation. They are deliberately wide and never presented as payroll truth.

02

Space need

People range × 90–115 square feet. Occupancy uses a $58–$82 gross range informed by the Colliers Toronto benchmark ↗. Address does not prove leased area.

03

Operating floor

$112k–$150k loaded people cost, 66%–79% delivery share, 1,325–1,500 collected hours and 12%–21% other operations. It is cost recovery—not a rate card.

04

Pricing evidence

Only a primary source counts as strong. Directory pricing is shown but discounted. The sole primary-source signal found is a City of Toronto record ↗ for Cossette's original 2017 contract.

precision is not the same thing as truth. a useful estimate shows its assumptions.

05Evidence standard

read the label before the number.

ReportedPublished by the agency, government, landlord or another primary source.

ObservedVisible in a public company profile or listing; useful, not audited.

ModelledCalculated as a range from public signals and stated inputs.

AssumedA normalized benchmark used to make unlike businesses comparable.

40%

average public-evidence coverage.

The score measures evidence found across entity and ownership (10 points), Toronto staffing (25), exact office footprint (20), role mix (20) and pricing (25). It is a completeness score—not a probability that an estimate is correct.
