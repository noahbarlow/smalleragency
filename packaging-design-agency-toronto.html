The pack is often the most important ad a consumer brand owns.

It has to carry the idea, explain the product, respect the regulations, survive a planogram and still look good in somebody's hand. That is not a decoration problem. It is a system problem.

We work from the shelf backwards. What needs to read first? What changes across flavours or formats? What can production repeat without slowly wrecking the brand? Those questions shape the design early, not after the hero mockup is approved.

01What a packaging system includes

the front is the smallest part of the job.

A packaging system is the set of decisions that keeps a product recognizable, understandable and producible across every SKU. It covers what shoppers see first, what changes across the range and how the design survives regulation, production and launch.

position

What can this brand own?

The useful difference that gives the design somewhere real to stand.

hierarchy

What must read first?

Brand, product, flavour, benefit and proof — in an order a shopper can actually follow.

range

What stays? What changes?

Rules for flavours, formats, sizes and channels that keep the family recognizable.

regulation

What has to fit?

Required copy, claims, tables, symbols and the less glamorous information that still needs a home.

production

How will it be made?

Dielines, substrates, colour, finishes, tolerances and files a supplier can use.

launch

Where does it show up next?

Retail, ecommerce, sell sheets, photography and campaign assets built from the same idea.

03Designed for Canadian shelves

the legal panel is still part of the design.

Canadian packaging gets crowded quickly. Bilingual copy, prescribed tables, claims, allergens, net quantity and barcodes cannot be treated like a production cleanup at the end.

We make room for the real information while the hierarchy is still being designed. Regulatory approval stays with the client and their qualified advisor; our job is to build a system that gives compliant content somewhere intelligent to live.

EN / FRbilingual from the start

Not an English design with French squeezed into whatever space survives.

NFtprescribed means prescribed

Nutrition Facts tables have their own format, type and placement requirements.

UPCleave the barcode alone

Size, contrast and quiet zones matter more than making it look decorative.

SKUdesign for the odd one

The longest flavour name and busiest panel are better tests than the easiest SKU.

Questions worth asking

before the first workshop.

The things founders and brand teams usually need to know before the brief is tidy.

What kinds of packaging do you design?

We focus on consumer packaging, especially food, beverage, supplements, wellness and other CPG categories. Projects range from one launch SKU to portfolio systems across many products.

What does a packaging design project include?

The scope can include category review, positioning, naming, visual identity, packaging architecture, design concepts, SKU navigation, production artwork, printer handoff and launch assets. We build the scope around what the brand actually needs.

Can you work with our existing brand identity?

Yes. Sometimes the identity is strong and the packaging system is the problem. We can work inside an existing brand, sharpen what is needed and avoid redesigning things that already work.

Do you handle production files and printer handoff?

Yes. The work is designed to be made, not just approved as a mockup. We support dielines, artwork systems, colour and finish direction, prepress coordination and supplier handoff.

Do you design for Canadian food labelling requirements?

Yes. We plan for bilingual copy, Nutrition Facts tables, ingredient and allergen information, net quantity, claims and barcodes from the beginning. Final regulatory approval remains with the client and their qualified regulatory partner.

Can the system handle a growing product range?

That is the point. We define what stays fixed, what changes and how shoppers navigate flavours, formats and sizes before the second, tenth or thirtieth SKU turns into a redesign problem.
