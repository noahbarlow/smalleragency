Peller Family Vineyards / Rebrand + packaging system

Change the pack.Keep the buyer.

French Cross was one of Canada’s biggest wines. We renamed it, rebuilt the system and carried the change across bottles, cartons and boxes without asking loyal buyers to start from zero.

01 / The risk

A big rebrand with no room for a dip.

At the time, French Cross sold more by litre than any other wine brand in Canada. People already knew the crest, the colour bands and where to find it.

The job was not to wipe the slate clean. It was to make the brand relevant to a younger audience without throwing away the memory that made it valuable.

02 / The method

Research did
the arguing.

We did not bet a national portfolio on taste.

The work moved through repeated consumer and competitive testing. Name, crest, hierarchy, photography and format were judged as one shelf system, not as isolated design decisions.

03 / Identity

Keep the memory.
Fix the machinery.

French Cross became Peller Family Vineyards. The crest was simplified. The masterbrand got clearer. The architecture made room for a much larger family.

It looked new, but the bones still felt familiar.

04 / Occasion

Ten varietals.
Ten useful reasons to pick one.

Each SKU got custom photography built around an occasion. No stock vineyard. No anonymous tasting ritual. The image helped people picture where the wine belonged.

05 / The system

One family.
Every format.

The idea had to work in the real world: 750 mL, 1.5 L, 1 L carton, 4 L box and a wide varietal range. Recognition came from structure. Choice came from colour and photography.

06 / What held

Years later,
the brand still leads.

Andrew Peller’s 2025 public filing describes Peller Family Vineyards as the leading brand in its IDB segment, known for strong consumer loyalty nationwide. It also remains the company’s most extensive portfolio, with 4L bag-in-box as its best-selling format.

A rebrand is never risk-free. But recognition can be designed into the change.

The useful lesson

Familiar does not have to mean frozen.

A rebrand can move a business forward without throwing away the memory that made it valuable. The trick is knowing which parts carry trust, then testing the change before the whole portfolio moves.
