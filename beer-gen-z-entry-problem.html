Thinking / Beer and culture

Beer isn’t being rejected.

It’s being skipped. That’s a much harder problem to see.

Field note / 4 minute read

The short version

Beer still behaves as if every new drinker already knows the language.

Gen Z does drink beer. The bigger issue is how often the category loses them before the first sip.

Beer is designed for people who already drink beer.

Styles, bitterness, heritage, glassware, brewery credibility. The category has built a rich language for people already inside it.

A new drinker arrives without the same familiarity, confidence or patience for the rules. Beer treats that gap like a lack of interest.

The first contact is a mismatch.

A new drinker needs clarity, low pressure and permission to participate. Beer often leads with seriousness, status cues and history.

The person is asking, “What is this, and is it for me?” The brand is answering, “Here’s our story since 1847.”

The order shouldn’t feel like an oral exam.

Saying the right thing. Knowing the style. Defending the choice. A simple social moment starts carrying unnecessary risk.

If confidence is required before comfort, people choose the category that asks less of them.

The whole path matters.

  1. 01
    Exposure

    Does the brand look like it belongs in the person’s world?

  2. 02
    Shelf

    Can they tell what it is and what it will feel like?

  3. 03
    Order

    Can they ask for it without needing the vocabulary?

  4. 04
    Sip

    Does the first taste reward the risk?

  5. 05
    Repeat

    Was the whole experience easy enough to become a habit?

Skipped categories rarely become habits.

Beer doesn’t need to be less beer. It needs a better first hello.
